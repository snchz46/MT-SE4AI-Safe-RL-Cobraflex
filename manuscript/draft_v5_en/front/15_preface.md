# Preface

This thesis is submitted as the Master's Thesis of the Automotive Systems (M.Sc.) programme of the Hochschule Esslingen, under the supervision of Prof. Dr.-Ing. Ralf Schüler. The work comes from one conviction: the responsible adoption of artificial intelligence in automotive safety functions does not only require capable algorithms, it also requires an engineering method that makes their behaviour traceable, verifiable and auditable. The methodological framework that these pages develop and apply to a concrete case was built on that premise.

**Motivation and origin.** The project started from a question that industrial practice asks before academic practice does: if a learned component cannot be specified in advance and cannot be verified against an expected answer, what is left of the V-Model? The answer defended here is not to abandon it, but to modify it at five concrete points and then to check whether the resulting cycle is still executable from end to end.

**Scope.** The study covers the range from hazard analysis and requirement derivation to the validation campaign in simulation and the characterisation of the gap towards the physical platform, on a single and deliberately bounded case study. The physical deployment is built, documented and **brought up on hardware** — the vehicle drives — but **the physical campaign has not been executed and no scenario has been scored on the real platform**: the physical evidence in this document is bring-up evidence, not verdict evidence; every run was made in monitoring mode, so the cage has never modified an action on the real vehicle, and the physical column of the consolidated requirements table is declared not executed for that reason. Where there is no measurement, the document says so instead of estimating it; where there is a measurement but not under protocol, it says that too.

**On the honesty of the reporting.** One decision runs through the whole work: uncomfortable results are reported as results. The global verdict of the reference campaign is literally `NOT SATISFIED`, one requirement is closed as not satisfied, and the conclusions chapter spends more space on what the framework did not demonstrate than on what it did. That choice is methodological: a traceability framework whose value consists in leaving no claim without evidence would lose its meaning if it were applied selectively to the favourable ones.
