# Abstract

The functional safety frameworks of the automotive industry, led by the ISO 26262 V-Model, assume that a component's behaviour can be specified in advance, checked against expected outputs and validated before deployment. Components trained with reinforcement learning (RL), a type of machine learning, break all three assumptions.

This thesis proposes and evaluates an adapted V-Model with five changes (A1–A5) that bring an RL component into a traceable lyfe cycle consistent with the norms and standarts: ISO 26262, ISO 21448 (SOTIF), ISO/IEC TR 5469 and UL 4600. The main contribution is a framework: an executable life cycle in which software checks the two-way traceability chain Hazard → Safety Requirement → Cage Rule → Scenario → Metric → Logged Evidence → Verdict. The framework is applied to a specific use case: lane following with a 1:14 scale vehicle (CobraFlex) trained in Gazebo. An end-to-end camera policy is contained by a safety cage of six deterministic rules that reads its own vision-based lane estimator, with a state-vector control arm.

The reference campaign (1,890 runs: 27 scenarios, with the cage filtering raw commands, enforcement mode, or only observing, monitoring mode) finds no road-edge contact inside the operational domain with the cage active, against 60 by the policy alone. The safety rules stay latent in regular driving and act where perception degrades. However, the rate limiter C-06, one of the cage rules, formally a non-critical smoothness rule, keeps the vehicle in the lane on the tightest curves: without the cage, the same policy leaves the road in 17 of 25 endurance runs, most likely through co-adaptation during training. The rule composition requirement (SR-010) is not met and is reported as a design limitation of the cage.

The sim-to-real gap is measured in steps up to the physical platform, where the work ended as a bring-up: no scenario was scored on hardware and the cage never changed an action on the real vehicle. It still showed what simulation could not: the camera's real field of view is 77.89°, not the 90° the simulator inherited; the validated policy does not transfer, having learned the 6.5:1 curve handedness of the training circuit as a steering bias; a policy retrained for transfer drove 18.05 m in one segment without triggering a safety rule (one run); and the lane estimator's accuracy depends on where the vehicle is on the circuit.

**Keywords:** reinforcement learning; autonomous driving; lane following; safety cage; functional safety; ISO 26262; SOTIF; SE4AI; scenario-based validation; traceability; sim-to-real gap; PPO; ROS2; Gazebo; CobraFlex.
